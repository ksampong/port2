Interests

Like many inspired by science fiction, I've always had a deep interest in Artificial Intelligence (AI). Throughout high school, I made several attempts to understand the field, with notable influences including 3Blue1Brown’s video series on machine learning and various popular science explainers. However, my understanding remained at a surface level. This past summer, I set out to develop a strong, practical understanding of the field, starting with the basics and working my way through the vast landscape of machine learning. I began by exploring the differences between machine learning, deep learning, neural networks, and other common AI buzzwords. Along the way, I realized how critical data science and data analysis were to this field. As I learned more, the landscape became clearer, and I was able to define a path for myself, as well as identify the destination I aimed to reach: understanding machine learning as a tool for problem-solving and analyzing complex systems.

First Attempt: Building a Neural Network from Scratch

My first practical attempt was to build a neural network from scratch—nothing fancy, just a proof of concept. My plan was simple: code a neuron, then a layer, and eventually a small network, based on what I had learned from YouTube. While creating the neuron and layer objects was straightforward, I quickly realized I didn’t know how to properly use them. Tasks like connecting neurons, passing signals from layer to layer, and adding cost functions proved more challenging than I had anticipated.

Fortunately, a friend introduced me to Kaggle, which turned out to be a game-changer.

Kaggle: The Turning Point

Kaggle provided a clear roadmap through its Kaggle Learn courses. I started with the introductory course on machine learning, where I was introduced to the pandas and scikit-learn libraries, and gained an understanding of data preparation. It was here that I realized machine learning was as much about analytics and statistics as it was about programming. I continued with the intermediate course, and while I found machine learning to be somewhat unintuitive, I became increasingly determined to master it because of its immense value. I also completed the pandas course to improve my data handling skills. Feeling ready for more, I moved on to deep learning, where I was introduced to TensorFlow and Keras. Finally, I was able to achieve my initial goal of building a functional neural network. I also explored computer vision through Kaggle's courses to further broaden my skills.

Internship Experience: Applying What I Learned

After my Kaggle courses, I wanted to apply what I had learned practically, so I began searching for internships. I landed a role with Stanbic (Standard) Bank Ghana in their software engineering department, where I was fortunate to work on an exploratory research project involving natural language processing (NLP). While I cannot disclose many details due to an NDA, my work focused on creating a neural network capable of extracting meaning from documents—a fascinating application of AI. Surprisingly, many of the tools I had learned, such as pandas and scikit-learn, weren’t used as much as I expected. Instead, we worked with a base neural network model and extended its functionality using specialized toolkits. The only time scikit-learn was useful was for data visualization, and even then, we opted for other solutions in the end. This experience deepened my understanding of machine learning and NLP in real-world applications, giving me a clearer vision of how to approach similar challenges in the future.

Quantum computing is currently my largest area of interest, and my short-term career goal is to contribute to the advancement of quantum processing and the development of quantum processing architectures.

Like with machine learning, I spent a lot of time in high school watching YouTube videos to understand quantum computing. However, I approached this subject much more seriously, aiming to grasp it mathematically and from a computer science perspective—anything that would give me a deeper understanding of the field. Early on, I realized I lacked some key fundamentals, particularly in linear algebra. So, I tried learning it through 3Blue1Brown’s content. Although the videos were insightful, I struggled to fully grasp the material.

This trial-and-error phase lasted for a while. I tried various resources, including IBM’s Fundamentals of Quantum Computing course, but often found myself losing focus or unable to internalize the information. This continued as I transitioned from high school to university.

First bit of Progress

In my second semester of university, I took an introductory linear algebra course, which reignited my interest in quantum computing. I began exploring my university’s quantum department and stumbled upon a paper on the Bloch sphere as a representation of a qubit. The paper felt surprisingly approachable, so I decided to reach out to one of the contributors, Professor Pereira, who happened to be at my university.

We had a productive discussion about quantum research, and he recommended a few books, including the well-known Quantum Computation and Quantum Information by Isaac Chuang and Michael Nielsen (often referred to as the "Mike and Ike" book). I attempted to read excerpts from it, but the content felt too advanced for me at the time. I decided to put it aside and focus on completing the semester.

Summer Study: Finding the Right Resources

As summer began, I revisited my quantum computing journey. After some research, I discovered a more approachable book: Introduction to Classical and Quantum Computing by Thomas Wong. I found this book particularly helpful, as it started with classical computing, covering topics from logic gates to Boolean algebra and encoding. It introduced me to VHDL and Verilog, and for the first time, I felt I had a solid understanding of computational complexity and error detection.

I enjoyed the clear sense of progression in Wong's book and set a goal for myself: to understand quantum computing well enough to apply for an internship and contribute meaningfully to any quantum project I joined. Around this time, I also signed up for IBM’s annual Qiskit Global Summer School.

Participating in the summer school was an incredibly educational experience. The labs were challenging, but I managed to complete them with support from other participants in the event's Discord server. My lab work is available on GitHub for those interested, though I admit it’s not perfect—it reflects my learning process during that time.

Current Focus and Future Goals

I’m currently continuing my study of the Wong textbook, albeit at a slower pace since school has resumed. My goal is to eventually tackle the Mike and Ike book once I feel more confident in my understanding.

I also have my sights set on a personal project: building an amateur physical qubit system. I plan to start this project once I feel capable enough in my quantum computing abilities.

Early Exploration: Raspberry Pi and Robotics

n Year 10, after years of anticipation, I finally got my first Raspberry Pi. My goal was to build a robotic arm, as I had always believed I was capable of it—what I needed were the right tools. I sourced some servos and jumper cables and repurposed an old desk lamp as the frame. The idea was simple: mount the servos to the hinges and base, providing three axes of movement. I learned to control the servos, but when it came time to assemble the arm, I encountered a challenge: the servos lacked the power to move the frame.

I tried various solutions, like removing the springs that added tension to the frame or considering stronger servos. Unfortunately, accessing additional parts was difficult at the time. Though I learned a lot through this experience, I had to shelve the project. In hindsight, while it taught me resourcefulness, I regretted not pushing further with other potential projects throughout high school. Nevertheless, I gained practical skills by experimenting with relay circuits, automating our home’s gate, and creating smart fan switches. My passion continued to grow despite the interruptions of attending a boarding school with limited access to technology.

Robotics Club and Early Internship

During high school, I had a brief stint with the robotics club, where we used LEGO Technic sets to explore various projects. Around the same time, I also interned briefly at Stanbic Bank Ghana, where I was involved in software automation using Python and the Selenium module, which deepened my interest in automation.

Though my progress was sporadic during this period, these experiences were invaluable in laying the foundation for the more serious projects I would take on later.

University and Design Challenges

At university, my interest in hardware was rekindled when I participated in the Teddy Bear Wheelchair Challenge for a design class during my first semester. The task was to design and build an autonomous wheelchair for a teddy bear, using Arduino, basic motors, and parts from Meccano sets. My team built one of the top-performing wheelchairs in the competition, which not only reignited my enthusiasm but also gave me hands-on experience with autonomous systems. /p>

Advancing My Skills: The 6502 Project and Summer Learning

That summer, I set an ambitious goal for myself: to build a working 6502 microprocessor while following Ben Eater’s YouTube series. Though other projects took priority, and I put the 6502 project on hold, I made progress by building 555 timer circuits in both astable and monostable configurations. These projects helped me sharpen my understanding of basic electronics and circuit design.

Current Focus: Deepening My Knowledge

Currently, I am working through The Art of Electronics by Horowitz and Hill, aiming for a steady progression in my knowledge. This book serves as my guide for refining my skills, and as I advance, I will continue documenting my journey in this journal.
